Score primarily on:
- Correctness of technical claims, not fluency in making them.
- Depth under follow-up: whether a confident first answer survived being pushed on.
- Resume claims: compare what the resume says with what the candidate could explain when
  asked. A claim explained with specifics is strong evidence. A claim they could not explain
  is a negative, however well it reads on paper.
- Code submitted during the round: correctness first, then edge cases, then complexity,
  then readability. Weigh submitted code above spoken description of code.
- Debugging and trade-off reasoning: can they say why, not only what.

Discount:
- Buzzword density and name-dropping of tools with no detail behind it.
- Polish of delivery. A hesitant correct answer beats a smooth wrong one.
- The closing questions the candidate asked at the end. They are not part of the score.

Treat as significant negatives:
- Confidently stated incorrect fundamentals.
- Coding answers that were never made to work and were not reasoned about.
- Contradicting an earlier answer without acknowledging it.

Calibrate to {{experience}}: do not penalise a mid-level candidate for lacking
architecture ownership, and do not reward a senior one for fundamentals alone.
